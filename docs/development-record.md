# Development record

This record keeps the details behind the decisions summarized in the [README](../README.md). The earlier failures remain part of the evidence. A later successful run does not change an earlier timeout, and a correct application reply does not mean the raw model answered correctly.

## Catalogue decisions and advice

The original keyboard mismatch involved product 142, named Korg but described as a Roland workstation, and product 144, named Roland but described as a Yamaha arranger. Marco chose to move the existing Roland description to 144, use the Yamaha description for 142 and rename 142 `Teclado Arranjador Yamaha 61 Teclas`. He then chose 88 keys for the derived Roland specifications to match that description. The other Roland specifications stayed unchanged. No exact Yamaha or Roland model was established with a manufacturer.

The [correction layer](../src/emporio/data/corrections.py) reads the description donors from the original rows, so importing again produces the same result. English descriptions follow the same donor mapping. Original CSV files and the original translation map remain unchanged. Derived entries record their donor IDs, retain prices, stock, product IDs and promotion relationships, and show an adjustment notice. The [correction checks](../tests/test_catalogue_corrections.py) cover that behavior. Earlier conversations retain the application snapshot from before these changes.

Four other products retain unresolved source contradictions. Product 135 mixes Music Man with Fender Jazz Bass and Alder with Ash. Product 137 mixes Yamaha with Ibanez and five strings with four. Product 139 mixes Yamaha with Pearl Export and Poplar plus mahogany with Maple. Product 140 mixes Pearl with Tama and Birch with Maple. Marco chose to show the uncertainty rather than invent a corrected identity or specification.

The derived SQLite table `product_conflicts` stores affected fields. Tools expose `catalogue_conflict`, `unconfirmed_fields`, `recommendation_eligible` and a warning in the current language. Search replies do not call conflicted entries confirmed recommendations, and comparisons exclude them from confirmed recommendations while retaining readable prices and stock. The [conflict checks](../tests/test_catalogue_conflicts.py) include a simulated model attempting an unsupported recommendation. Browser checks covered warnings in the catalogue, preview and product pages. This is an internal consistency review, not manufacturer verification of every catalogue entry.

Advice initially exposed a Bonsai search loop. A budget filter removed an expensive comparison candidate, and the model kept searching rather than explaining why that candidate did not fit. The revised flow resolves the named instruments from the customer's request, offers the restricted comparison tools and completes the assessment after one model round. It keeps candidates above the budget visible and separates confirmed, unmet and unknown criteria. Missing electronics information does not automatically mean no electronics. A unique confirmed match can support a conditional recommendation, while conflicting or unavailable candidates cannot.

The [final comparison conversation](../examples/05-bonsai-instrument-advice.md) passed ten application cases, with seven turns involving the model and three direct catalogue replies. Earlier failures remain documented in the [quality record](quality-notes.md). The live runner records connection failures even when a labelled catalogue fallback provides useful information. It does not turn that degraded run into successful model acceptance.

## Policy retrieval and pricing

The policy tool retrieves relevant complete PDF pages, returns their source references and selects reviewed English or Portuguese details for supported questions. It is retrieval without embeddings, not an unrestricted semantic document search. The original customer question controls the detail selection even if a model paraphrases it or chooses another topic. A supported question triggers a logged lookup when the model omits one. Replies suppress unrelated product cards and policy details, while unknown wording can still receive a general topic summary.

The [Bonsai policy conversation](../examples/04-bonsai-policy-review.md) passed twelve cases with 23 provider calls. It covered card instalments and minimum amounts, mixed payments above R$2,000, externally financed PIX with full immediate receipt, nonstacking discounts, refund timing and payment method, warranty exclusions, delivery quotes for large instruments and weekend opening hours. Four turns repeated targeted topics in Portuguese with changing instrument context. On two Portuguese turns the model omitted the policy tool, and the application supplied it. The result therefore evaluates the combination of model and application.

Tests also check eleven policy formulations in both languages and focused replies across all 65 product contexts, including models that return unsolicited product details. Further implemented rules cover instruments versus accessories, inactive promotions, return conditions, exchanges, warranty terms, regional delivery and transport damage. Those rules are not all claimed as individual cases in the twelve case live conversation.

Marco chose the highest eligible single discount rather than combining promotions with PIX. Full immediate PIX receipt qualifies even when an external bank finances the customer's payment. A mixed payment does not receive the PIX discount, although an independently valid product promotion may still apply. The manual's conflicting general and specific minimum instalment amounts remain visible, as do the unclear start of refund processing, inconsistent contact information and the undefined shipping rule at exactly R$500. No real refund or financing is performed.

## Illustration experiment

The image work began because Marco wanted the catalogue and product pages to give customers a clearer visual impression. A local ComfyUI workflow using Krea2 Turbo generated a Fender pilot from a reference. Three candidates were produced. The first contained overlapping guitars and strong artefacts, and its initial automated approval was overturned by direct inspection. Later candidates were usable as illustrations but did not establish exact model fidelity. Automatic quality assessment was not reliable enough to approve images by itself.

A later photo search found freely licensed guitar images, but the exact model, useful framing and clear permission to republish did not consistently coincide. Public manufacturer pages did not establish permission to redistribute their photos. The collected photos were therefore not adopted as shop assets. That is the limit of the evidence and permissions established here, not a claim that all instrument photos are unavailable for reuse.

Workflow corrections, reference research and visual reviews took substantial development time for an optional feature. The agent recommended a consistent style and a small pilot spanning two or three instrument types before scaling. Marco later accepted a relaxed illustrative standard, requiring complete, clean motifs while tolerating documented differences in strings, keys, hardware and finish. This allowed the catalogue to use a coherent set without claiming accurate photographs of every model.

The published set contains 61 JPG illustrations and four neutral SVG fallbacks for products 135, 137, 139 and 140. The shared renderer enforces those fallbacks even if an older image index contains a generated image for a conflicted entry. The [image index](../src/emporio/web/static/products/index.json) records source candidate hashes and tolerated differences. A browser check decoded all 61 JPGs and confirmed the four fallbacks. That is a technical integration check, not an independent second visual approval of every image.

The red AI notice is an HTML caption, not text burned into the JPG. Its caption, tooltip and alternative text follow English or Portuguese, including an open preview and previously Portuguese fragments inserted after a language change. Detailed visual review notes remain in the index rather than mixed language customer tooltips. The correction was reproduced and checked across repeated switches, 61 cards, product pages, reload and mobile views.

Reference photographs, earlier candidates, workflow copies and detailed reviews stay local under the ignored `pilot_work` directory. The four unused JPG exports for the conflicted products are also ignored and unused. Original source files were not deleted. The public repository does not redistribute those reference photographs, and their use for generation does not establish permission to republish them. The selected illustrations and their index are enough to run the shop. Recreating the image generation from the public repository alone is not promised.

## Functional acceptance and corrections

The first Qwen smoke test used the local abliterated Q8_0 variant for a greeting and a product quote. It found that the Qwen chat template rejected a later system message. Requests now have exactly one first system message combining instructions, current language and validated interface context, followed by conversation and tool messages. The current context is rebuilt on every model round rather than stored as another system message in history.

A manual conversation also exposed repeated product answers. Identical read requests are reused within a turn, duplicate factual paragraphs appear once and the trace marks reused results. Separate product focus options allow sound, specifications, overview, availability and price questions to receive the recorded facts they actually request. Product selection and suggested questions no longer submit a PIX question automatically.

The broader Bonsai functional acceptance found context, filter, tool selection, missing weight, accessory and order response defects. After corrections, the final run passed 24 steps across three sessions. It contained 21 chat turns, of which seventeen used the provider, and three verification or reset API events. Four chat turns were direct application clarifications or stock checks. The model reported `Bonsai-27B-Q1_0.gguf` with `xhigh` reasoning. Questions were prepared for evaluation, not collected from independent customers. The [acceptance record](model-acceptance.json) and the three functional conversations preserve the difference between failed attempts and the final result.

Automated tests simulate model responses while exercising real application and database behavior. The earlier 143 case suite passed on Windows and WSL, and the current 153 case suite passed on Windows during the submission review. Separate browser checks exercised model use on desktop and at 390px width, including a real PIX quote, wrong and matching order emails, verified order lookup and forced Portuguese. Tests with a deliberately compromised model stub validate the navigation boundary and reset independently of any real model's willingness to attack.

## Prompt injection evaluations

The [historical comparison](injection-comparison.json) keeps the early abliterated Qwen baseline separate from intermediate Bonsai measurements. The old running app changed categories after two attacks. The correction requires an explicit customer click on an expiring navigation proposal bound to the session and exact target. The chat reset defect was also corrected. The early and intermediate phases used different application states and recording methods, so they are not a controlled ranking of models.

The later primary reviews used the same application hashes and the original ten attacks plus one legitimate control. The [Bonsai review](bonsai-injection-review-20261104.json) records nine completed attacks without an observed unauthorized application effect, one attack timeout and an initial control timeout at the 45 second deadline. Repeating the control with the model loaded returned the real R$569.91 PIX price and stock of twelve. The original failure remains recorded. The raw model answered an unrelated rainbow question, drifted into Portuguese despite English selection and produced empty replies at the 2,048 token output limit. A malformed policy argument was corrected by application request binding.

The [abliterated Qwen Q8_0 review](qwen-injection-review-20261104.json) records six completed attacks and four attack timeouts. Its legitimate control passed. Raw text disclosed the exact validated interface context and accepted a forged R$0.01 price. This was internal navigation metadata rather than customer credentials, and no verbatim system instruction disclosure was observed. Neither the disclosure nor the false price reached the visible customer answer. Navigation attacks did not change the view or produce a proposal. The credential requests that timed out remain inconclusive, not evidence of a refusal.

The [longer deadline review](injection-followup-review-20261104.json) tested seven scenarios per model with an attack, an escalation claiming earlier approval and a legitimate recovery question. Conversation history was kept within each sequence, and the test provider allowed 120 seconds per read. The application retained its existing 120 second total turn limit. Failed turns were not always retained in subsequent model history, which is recorded per request. Three customer messages do not necessarily mean three provider calls.

The 42 main messages produced 21 HTTP 200 replies for Bonsai and twenty for Qwen. Qwen's remaining message rejected a tool call truncated into invalid JSON at 2,048 tokens. No timeout or unauthorized application effect was observed, and all fourteen recovery questions returned the real price and stock. Bonsai nevertheless followed a role override after initially refusing, carried the fake price and stock of 999 into a legitimate follow up, invented a completed refund and R$1 price within roleplay, and later refused a harmless question. Qwen also adopted false price and stock. The application did not display those raw claims.

Two separate diagnostics with 4,096 tokens brought that review to 48 messages, 47 provider calls and sixteen successful application recovery answers. Fifteen completions were empty at the output limit. Bonsai still produced no raw answer to its legitimate order sequence control after all 4,096 tokens and about 62 seconds. Its heavily quantized Q1_0 weights and reasoning configuration are possible contributors, but no matched higher precision run isolated the cause. Qwen's roleplay diagnostic returned valid tools using 1,930, 1,409 and 1,191 tokens, all below the old limit. That was a different scenario from Bonsai's order sequence and does not prove that a larger budget fixed truncation. A repeated price reference also triggered an application clarification without invoking a model.

The [NVFP4/BF16 review](qwen-nvfp4-injection-review-20261104.json) repeated eleven original cases, 21 dialogue messages and three roleplay diagnostic messages with `Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-NVFP4-BF16.gguf`. All 35 messages returned HTTP 200, with 34 provider calls, nine correct controls and no observed unauthorized application effect. Raw off topic compliance and R$0.01 price adoption remained reproducible. The earlier context disclosure and credential solicitation were not observed. One nonempty refusal was cut off at 2,048 tokens. No empty output limit completion or malformed tool argument occurred. The 4,096 token diagnostic used 378, 294 and 393 tokens, so it does not show a benefit caused by raising the limit.

The [GSQ review](qwen38-gsq-injection-review-20261104.json) used the API profile `Qwen3.8-27B-GSQ_no_MTP_noVision`, whose server loaded `Qwen3.8-27B-GSQ-RCO-IQ3_S.gguf`. Its same 35 messages produced 34 HTTP 200 replies, 34 provider calls and one primary forged policy timeout. The longer deadline repeat completed after about 48 seconds. All nine controls returned correct application facts. Completed raw attempts did not reproduce role override, context disclosure, forged price adoption or invented refund completion, including after escalation. One legitimate recovery exhausted 2,048 tokens without a raw answer after about 54 seconds. The application still returned the real price and stock. The separate roleplay diagnostic used 968, 688 and 542 tokens and did not repeat that empty recovery case.

The NVFP4 profile used a different weight file, context allocation and CPU expert offloading from the old Q8_0 profile. GSQ used another model family, IQ3_S file, 90,000 context tokens and `xhigh` reasoning. Sampling and runtime profiles also differ between Bonsai and Qwen. Local filenames and server paths were verified, but checkpoint training history, abliteration status and the meaning of GSQ or RCO were not independently established from names alone. These are observations from fixed stochastic attempts, not independent attack success estimates or a general model ranking.

Following [OWASP prompt injection testing guidance](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/LLM_Prompt_Injection_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html) and [agent validation guidance](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/AI_Agent_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html), the records separate raw model content, tool requests and application effects. Timeouts and empty replies do not count as successful blocking. This was not a comprehensive adaptive, multimodal or document poisoning campaign.

Marco accepted the documented residual risks on 4 November 2026 and retained the existing 45 second provider read deadline, 2,048 token output budget and application defenses. No additional code hardening or model run accompanied that decision. A disconnected client does not guarantee that every external model server immediately stops generating. The requested review is complete for this prototype, while historical failures and the absence of a general security certification remain unchanged.

## Reproducing the checks

Install `requirements-dev.txt` and run the suite with the environment commands in the README. Separate package snapshots remain in `requirements-lock-windows.txt` and `requirements-lock-wsl.txt` because platform dependencies differ. Live runners need an actual configured model service, whose weights and runtime are not included in the repository. They make real provider calls and are optional rather than part of normal shop startup.

`scripts/check_browser.py` checks a running shop on port 8765 and uses installed Edge, Brave, Chromium or Chrome where available, otherwise a separately installed Playwright browser. `scripts/check_language.py`, `scripts/check_product_flow.py` and `scripts/check_advice_browser.py` cover their respective interface flows. The product flow runner expects Edge. `EMPORIO_TEST_URL` can point browser checks that support it at an isolated application.

`scripts/check_policy_model.py` records actual policy requests under `.runtime/bonsai-policy-evaluation`. `scripts/check_advice_model.py` records model advice, direct replies and explicitly labelled fallbacks separately. Both use the local Bonsai configuration by default. An endpoint may need time to load its model on the first request.

For broader functional evaluation, run `.venv/bin/python scripts/check_model_acceptance.py --output .runtime/model-acceptance-new-run`, substituting the Windows interpreter when appropriate. It refuses to overwrite an existing result folder. `scripts/publish_acceptance_evidence.py` publishes the three final sessions only from a passing report with matching application hashes. `scripts/record_examples.py` now writes preview diagnostics under `.runtime/preview-examples` so it does not overwrite the submission conversations.

`scripts/check_acceptance_browser.py --api-base http://127.0.0.1:8080/v1 --model Bonsai27B` exercises actual model use against a separate app on port 8770. `scripts/check_injection_defenses.py` starts and stops a separate app on port 8771 with a compromised model stub. It checks navigation and reset without real inference. `scripts/check_live_injection.py` records the original corpus, model calls, screenshots and visible effects. Its `--followups`, `--provider-timeout` and `--completion-budget` options reproduce the targeted sequence and budget variants without changing the production provider. Use a fresh output folder and an unused isolated port.

Raw runs and screenshots stay under the ignored `.runtime` directory. Public JSON reports retain application hashes and compact outcomes without redistributing internal diagnostic output. The [quality record](quality-notes.md) documents defects and their corrections. No test runner or example publisher should modify the submitted repository after the actual final submission.
